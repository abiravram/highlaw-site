FAQ

Have a question? We're happy to help

I was injured at work — who do I sue?

By definition, a "work accident" is an event in which a person is injured while performing their work for their employer. If you were injured at work, first contact the National Insurance Institute to secure your full entitlements. If the accident resulted from employer negligence, a claim may also be filed against the employer and against the business's insurer.

How long do I have to sue an insurance company after a car accident?

An insurance company can be sued within 7 years of the date of the accident. For a minor, the limitation period only begins once the minor turns 18 (i.e. 7 years from their 18th birthday).

What's the difference between "ownership" and "leasehold", and between "property rights" and "contractual rights"?

Ownership incorporates every possible right in a property. A leasehold, by contrast, can be broad and extensive, but never includes full ownership, and by nature preserves a connection to the property for the owner. A lease can include terms such as lease payments and duration, while ownership is not limited by such conditions.

I'm about to rent an apartment — why pay a lawyer when I can download a contract online?

A lease is the legal basis for your right to occupy an apartment while the landlord remains the owner. It's important to know who you're renting from, their track record with previous tenants, and to make sure the lease terms work for you. A lawyer reviewing the contract at a reasonable cost can prevent significant losses later.

I want to rent out my apartment and downloaded a lease template online — what's the risk?

The contract itself matters, but the tenant's identity, background, history with other landlords, ability to honor their obligations, and above all the guarantees you can secure — all matter far more. Once the property is handed over, it may take a long time to get it back, so a proper legal review is worth the investment.

How long does an urban renewal ("pinui-binui") project take?

The timeline depends on cooperation between the property owners, the local authority and the developer. Where the authority has a clear urban-renewal policy and residents are engaged while the developer moves quickly, timelines shorten significantly. In practice, different projects have taken anywhere from about two years to nearly a decade, depending on the specific circumstances.

What does "notarized translation" mean?

It is a translation of a document, performed by a notary, who certifies that the translation is faithful and identical to the original.
